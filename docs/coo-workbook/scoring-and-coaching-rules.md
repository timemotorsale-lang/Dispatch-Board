# COO Workbook Scoring and Coaching Rules

Status: supplemental source of truth

Purpose: define how the workbook measures daily effectiveness without creating a surveillance-like experience.

## Scoring Philosophy

Scores are guidance, not grades. They exist to help staff and managers answer:

- Is the day clear?
- Is work moving?
- Are blockers being raised early?
- Is communication current?
- Is tomorrow prepared?

Scores should not be used as standalone performance judgments.

## Score Families

### Focus Score

Measures planning clarity.

Signals:

- Daily Focus outcomes exist.
- Top outcomes are limited and clear.
- Must-close items are identified.
- Priority and risk are defined.
- Support needs are named early.

### Execution Score

Measures progress against planned work.

Signals:

- Daily Focus items completed.
- Checklist items completed.
- Formal task conversions when needed.
- Unfinished items have update notes or carryover state.

### Communication Score

Measures whether meaningful updates were captured.

Signals:

- Quick Updates entered for unplanned work.
- Follow-ups include due window or owner context.
- Report Seeds have review notes when needed.
- Manager/support notes exist for blocked work.

### Risk Score

Measures how much unresolved risk remains.

Signals:

- Daily Focus watch/blocked items
- checklist yellow/red items
- Quick Update follow-ups
- missing due windows
- open support actions
- operating exceptions

### Carryover Quality Score

Measures whether unfinished work is prepared for tomorrow.

Signals:

- Carryovers are created from real sources.
- Carryovers are confirmed or intentionally dismissed.
- Must-close items not completed are visible.
- Tomorrow setup is clear.

## Coaching Language Rules

Staff-facing prompts should be:

- brief
- specific
- action-oriented
- supportive
- focused on the next step

Examples:

- Add your top three outcomes before work gets busy.
- One must-close item needs a decision before close.
- Add a due window to the customer follow-up.
- Ask for support on the blocked parts item.
- Confirm tomorrow ownership for the roof follow-up.

Avoid:

- blame language
- ranking staff against each other
- implying laziness
- exposing raw surveillance data
- long coaching paragraphs inside the workflow

## Manager Summary Rules

Manager-facing summaries should be direct and useful:

- Alex completed 5 of 6 planned items.
- One red blocker needs assignment support.
- Two carryovers need tomorrow ownership.
- Quick Updates are current.
- No daily focus was set today.

Manager summaries should preserve context and encourage support.

## Strength Signals

The system should surface positive patterns:

- starts day with clear outcomes
- closes most planned work
- raises blockers early
- captures unplanned work
- confirms carryovers
- converts work into formal tasks when needed
- improves over repeated days

## Coaching Opportunity Signals

The system should surface coachable patterns:

- repeated carryovers
- no start-of-day focus
- late blocker discovery
- follow-ups without due windows
- report seeds needing cleanup
- too many planned priorities
- support needs not raised
